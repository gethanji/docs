handbook / product

What Hanji is

Hanji is a workspace with three surfaces. Think gives ideas room on a canvas. Plan turns the next step into an issue, project or objective. Document keeps knowledge in real Markdown. They share a workspace and a standing conversation with the conductor. This is the first step toward our mission: shared understanding on a foundation you own. Read Mission and vision for the direction, or From idea to action for a worked example.

The current app is an invited desktop preview. The public handbook is available to everyone; public app downloads and hosted sync are not included.

Start where the work is

Use Think canvas for notes, diagrams, tables, templates and workshops. Objects can become issues in Plan or a page in Document. Use Objectives and key results to connect a goal to the work behind it, and Reading and writing to preserve what the team learns. Desktop workspace explains setup and navigation.

Keep the files under your control

Repositories and local folders become mounts in the workspace. Document reads and edits Markdown in place. Plan keeps file-backed issues and projects. Think accepts revisioned changes into a live journal and makes automatic Git checkpoints. The products’ storage boundaries differ; they are not all a disposable cache.

Back up durable workspace configuration and session/canvas journals alongside your repositories. The index can be rebuilt; accounts, grants, comments and uncheckpointed accepted edits cannot simply be recovered from that index. Mounts and Think canvas describe the details.

Bring an agent when it helps

Connect your own Claude or Codex setup. The conductor carries the current page, issue or selected canvas objects into the conversation. It can act within your rights and token scopes, ask for required approval, or propose an edit for review. Supported changes have Undo. A model subscription is separate and ordinary reading, writing and canvas work do not require one.

The conductor, Agents and Proposals cover the agent surfaces.

Respect the access boundary

Pages, search, links, exported content and agent tools follow the access model. A shared workspace does not make private content public. Permissions explains which checks apply to each surface, including the limits of a local desktop owner session and direct access to the underlying repository.

Hanji’s aim is a calm human surface and useful agent access without taking ownership of the work. Why Hanji is different explains those choices; Features lists what the current preview implements.
