The story

Room to think. Clarity to move.

Hanji started with knowledge: real Markdown that people could read and improve, and coding agents could use through a scoped surface. The files should remain useful after the app is gone, and the human reading experience should matter as much as the agent protocol.

Working that way brought the next question into view. An idea often starts as fragments on a canvas, becomes a next step, then leaves behind knowledge someone else will need. Those moments should stay connected.

Three surfaces, one workspace

Think makes room for notes, diagrams and conversation. Plan gives the next step an owner and a place among projects and objectives. Document keeps the context in Markdown. They share a workspace rather than asking people to rebuild the same identity and background in each tool.

The conductor is a standing agent conversation across those surfaces. It carries context, follows access scopes, asks for required approval and makes supported changes reversible. It is optional: people can read, write and organize without connecting a model account.

Files that outlast the interface

Document and Plan keep file-backed content; Think combines a live edit journal with automatic Git checkpoints. Durable configuration and journals need backup alongside the repositories. The promise is control over your work, with those boundaries named honestly.

A calm surface and a clear lock

The interface should give the work attention rather than demand it. Shared navigation, cool neutral surfaces, useful editing controls and readable history are part of that aim. So is scoped access: a person or agent should see only what the workspace permits, with direct repository access explained as a separate limit.

The current app remains an invited desktop preview. We are learning through the working product rather than promising a hosted service or a finished public release.

Why “Hanji”

한지 is Korean paper. The name expresses a hope that the work will outlast its interface. The name tells that story; What Hanji is describes today’s workspace.
