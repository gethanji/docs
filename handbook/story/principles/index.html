Principles

A short list of the beliefs Hanji is built on. When a decision is hard, these break the tie.

  • Clarity supports judgment. Make context, contributions and choices understandable so people can question an agent's work and decide what happens next.
  • Keep the interface thin. Give each interaction the surface it needs. The tool should help people understand and direct the work.
  • Learn through use. Build working experiments, try them in real work and keep iterating as tools and methods change.
  • Share the results. Publish the research and its open-source results so others can examine, use and build on the work.
  • Git holds portable content. Document keeps Markdown in the repository and its index is rebuildable. Accounts, grants and Document comments live in durable configuration. The separate Think canvas journals accepted live edits before automatic Git checkpoints; its journal is durable too. A clone carries the checkpoint, not uncheckpointed edits.
  • The human never meets git. Reading and writing should feel like a good editorial tool. Commits, branches, and conflicts are plumbing, and plumbing stays behind the wall until someone goes looking for it.
  • Agents are first-class readers. Hanji treats an agent as a reader with an identity and a scope, the same as a person, and lets it propose rather than overwrite.
  • No bundled intelligence. We do not sell you a model or meter your tokens. Bring the agent you already trust. Your bill does not grow because your team wrote more this month.
  • Permissions are honest about their limits. We say plainly what a lock protects and what it leaves open. A promise we cannot keep is worse than no promise.
  • Byte-fidelity. A one-word edit produces a one-line change. Your history stays readable because the tool refuses to rewrite what you did not touch.

None of these is novel on its own. Holding all of them at once, and refusing to trade one away for a feature, is the point.
